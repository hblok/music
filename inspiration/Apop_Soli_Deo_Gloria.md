# Apoptygma Berzerk — *Soli Deo Gloria* (1993): the track-by-track blueprint

*Companion to `EBM_1990s.md` — that doc covers the genre and targets the
1996–2000 futurepop hinge; this one pins down the **1993 dark-electro
Apop** so its sound can be captured on its own terms. Written
2026-09-05. Facts marked **[verified]** come from the sources listed at
the end (Discogs credits, the band's own interviews and reissue notes,
two long-form reviews). Everything else is characterisation from the
era, the gear and the reviews — **no audio was measured**; §7 lists the
inspector runs that settle each open number.*

---

## 1. The record [verified]

| | |
|---|---|
| Released | 11 November 1993, Tatra Records, TATCD 018 (CD, 8-page booklet) |
| Length | 47:09, 13 tracks |
| Produced & arranged | Stephan Groth + **Erik Wøllo** (tracks 1–6, 8–13); Groth alone on *Arp* |
| Groth | vocals, sampler, programming, all writing (except the Lou Reed cover) |
| Guests | **Anders Odden** — additional guitar on *Burnin' Heretic* |
| Studios | recorded/mixed at Wintergarden Studios; *Arp* recorded at "Apoptygma Sound Lab" (Groth's home setup) in 1992, "refreshed" at Wintergarden 1993 |
| Mastering | Morten Lund, Masterhuset (1993); the same engineer remastered from the original tapes in July 2018 (Lunds Lyd, Oslo) |
| Artwork | an Albrecht Dürer woodcut; design Halvor Bodin |
| Singles | *Ashes to Ashes* (23 April 1991, B-side *Wrack 'em to Pieces*); *Bitch* (1 May 1993) |
| Status | "the very first Norwegian EBM album" (Side-Line); no chart in Norway, broke the band in Germany (first tour 1994); Discogs community 4.13/5 |

**The title** is Latin for "glory to God alone" — Groth's Christian
background frames the record (the woodcut, the lyrics on faith and
mortality, the heretic-burning imagery), yet the reviews agree it is
"enjoyable by people of all backgrounds" and never preachy.

**The gear** (Groth, Electricity Club interview): *"I had a Roland
Juno 60, a Roland SH101 and an Ensoniq EPS sampler which cost a
fortune!"* — the EPS being *"13 bit and mono… you really had to work on
truncating the samples and making them as small as possible to fit the
memory."* Sequenced on *"an Atari running either Cubase or Pro24 with 24
MIDI tracks."* Earliest work lived on Atari floppies and cassette, DAT
for final masters. *"I was young and hungry and had no clue what I was
doing, but everything was fun."* On the remaster: *"From all the
limitations we had, it's actually quite impressive what we got out of
it."*

**The influences** (Groth, same interview): The Velvet Underground, The
Invincible Spirit, Front 242, Nitzer Ebb, the German labels Music
Research and Zoth Ommog; of the North American scene only Psyche. Not
Nine Inch Nails ("an alternative rock act").

**Groth's own picks:** *"Burning Heretic and Backdraft are probably the
best two songs from the album, they've both become club classics in
Germany and the US."*

**The reviews, condensed:** goth-dance fused with EBM; "dark rhythmic
grooves with heavy emphasis on life-and-death matters"; the vocals
alternate "between doom-laden and scary-evil" over "cruel-sounding
electronic pulses"; choruses catchy enough to suggest "traditionally
oriented songwriting" (Depeche Mode comparisons on *Bitch*); grooves
never overstay; "Gloomy, but not creepy; dark, but not abysmal." The
short instrumental pieces "really help with the flow… make it work as a
complete piece — rare on a full-length debut."

## 2. The 1993 palette — what the gear forces

Three instruments and a sequencer. That constraint IS the sound, and it
maps one-to-one onto synthesis:

- **SH-101 = the bassline.** One oscillator (saw, square, sub-octave
  square), a resonant 24 dB lowpass with envelope, the built-in
  sequencer. Every mechanical bassline on the record is this: gated
  8ths or 16ths, filter-envelope pluck, a touch of resonance, mono,
  centre, dry. It is also the zap/lead machine (the SH-101's
  filter-self-oscillation blips).
- **Juno-60 = the pads, stabs and arps.** DCO saw + sub-osc, the
  famous chorus (I/II), a built-in arpeggiator (up/down/random, 1–3
  octaves) — the obvious source of *Arp*, and of the minor-chord
  goth pads and chorused stabs that carry the "goth-dance" half.
  Polyphony six voices, so pads are thin (2–3 note voicings).
- **Ensoniq EPS = the drums, the hits, the voices, the noise.** 13-bit
  mono, tiny memory: every drum is a short truncated sample (hard
  transients, no tails), orchestral/choir hits are chopped to a
  fraction of a second, film/speech snippets are grainy and short.
  The aliasing and the abrupt sample ends are audible and are *the*
  1993 texture. Reverb is added at the desk, not baked in.
- **Atari + Cubase = rigid quantisation.** No swing, no humanising —
  the stiffness is deliberate and reads as EBM discipline.
- **Plus:** one real guitar riff (Odden, *Burnin' Heretic*), Erik
  Wøllo's production hand (a Norwegian ambient guitarist/synthesist
  — expect his touch in the atmospheric interludes; inference), and
  Groth's voice in two modes (§5).

**What is NOT here** (1993, Norway, three synths): no supersaw, no
trance arps wall, no big string library, no sidechain pump, no
sub-bass layer — the low end is the SH-101 and the sampled kick.

## 3. The songs

Detected BPM/key values come from algorithmic tags on the 2018
remaster (Beatport / GetSongBPM) — **approximate**, some conflicting;
§7 measures them. Durations are the 1993 CD [verified].

### Bitch — 4:24 · ≈120 BPM · C♯ minor (detected)
The single (May 1993) and the album's calling card. "Hard-hitting,
dismissive" — a swipe at an ex, "assembled with sufficient maturity";
this is where the Depeche Mode comparison lands: a real verse/chorus
song over EBM machinery.
- **Mechanism to capture:** the *slow* stomper. 120 is the album's
  floor tempo for a dance track — heavier, more deliberate than the
  147 of *Backdraft*. Sequenced SH-101 bass in 8ths on a pedal,
  sampled kick and slam-snare on every beat / 2&4, a chorused Juno
  stab on the offbeat, the sung chorus as the hook (the title word,
  expected). Sneer in the verse, melody in the chorus.
- **Repo shape:** song form at 120, C♯ or A minor; refrain voice
  clean; a *dismissive* vocal attitude translates to a clipped,
  detached refrain rhythm (short notes, rests) rather than legato.

### Burnin' Heretic (Album Version) — 5:33 · 129 BPM (GetSongBPM; a 114 tag also exists) · key unmeasured
Groth's pick and a club classic; "the definitive version" for live
sets. Existed in at least three cuts: the 1992 *Crisp Version* (6:11,
on the *Sex, Drugs & EBM* sampler), the album version, and a *Gothic
Version*. The album version's melodies are "lighter in tone allowing
the rhythmic elements to drive the song forward", with "space in the
mix for a thrashy guitar riff" (Anders Odden).
- **Mechanism to capture:** the rhythm-driven anthem — melody thinned
  on purpose so the bass/drum engine leads; the guitar riff as a
  *texture* stab, not a lead. The song was reworked until the rhythm
  won: the lesson is subtractive.
- **Repo shape:** the driving 128–130 stomper; the "guitar" slot is
  either a declared new voice (a tanh-driven saw-stack power-chord
  gate in the E2–E3 register, palm-mute short) or simply left to the
  bass. Hook count high — this is the one you chant.

### Stitch — 3:06 · tempo unmeasured (the Clock DVA remix sits at 95)
"Slow, seething electro-industrial" with harsh vocals ("never this
project's strong point" — but the *texture* is the point). Short, no
chorus payoff; the album's darkest three minutes. Clock DVA remixed
it — a lineage marker: this is the Zoth Ommog / dark-electro side.
- **Mechanism to capture:** the half-time seethe: sparse kick, a big
  slam on 3, a filtered noise/bass drone under everything, the
  distorted vocal used as a *rhythmic bark*, a Juno pad in a dark
  minor cluster. Tension without release.
- **Repo shape:** the trough piece. The harsh-vocal slot is filled by
  a distorted formant bark — a gated, tanh(2.0) saw with two formant
  peaks, rhythmic not melodic (instrumental rule: no voice, but
  the *shape* of a bark is fair game).

### Backdraft — 4:20 · ≈147 BPM · C♯ minor (detected)
Groth's other pick, the club classic. "Jackhammering drums and
spiritual musings." The fastest thing on the record. An alternate
*Sarpsborg Synth Version* (Sarpsborg = Groth's home town) from the
sessions surfaced on the 2018 CD.
- **Mechanism to capture:** the jackhammer. At 147 a 16th bass is
  frantic — expect 8ths on the SH-101 with the *drums* carrying the
  16th energy (double-kick figures, machine-gun snare runs, closed
  hats every 16th). Tempo as aggression; the vocal rides above it
  half-sung, half-declaimed.
- **Repo shape:** the fast EBM track — 147, C♯ minor (fits the
  detection), the gallop cell on the bass, snare rolls as a *groove*
  device not just a seam. A different animal from the 120 stomper —
  don't blend them into one 136 compromise.

### Spiritual Reality — 4:38 · ≈109 BPM · F♯ minor (detected)
"Hammering beat and mechanical bassline" in the manner of Project
Pitchfork; Groth's "musings about his own faith and mortality".
- **Mechanism to capture:** the mid-tempo hammer — the Pitchfork
  school: ~110, huge dry snare, kick on every beat, a mechanical
  8th-note bass that never leaves the root, a slow dark pad, the
  vocal low and solemn. The weight comes from the tempo, not density.
- **Repo shape:** the "hammer" archetype; verify the 109 (a 218/2
  read would be unusual but check the per-30 s table).

### Skyscraping (Schizophreniac) — 7:13 · unmeasured
The longest track, a sample collage: Discogs credits fourteen
sampled performers — Alice In Chains, Angst Pop, Biosphere, Die
Krupps, Entombed, Front 242, Germ, PJ Harvey (*Rid of Me*), Pixies,
Ride, Sonic Youth, The Fair Sex, Vomito Negro, Yello. It "switches
between multiple rhythmic patterns and noise sections" (Only
Solitaire); the listener's guide calls it the one weak track,
"half-developed ideas". The subtitle is the form.
- **Mechanism to capture:** the suite — hard cuts between grooves,
  noise sections as seams, a collage aesthetic (the EPS as the
  instrument). Historically interesting (grunge and shoegaze samples
  inside an EBM record, 1993 Norway); as a *model* it is the machine
  score shape, not the song shape.
- **Repo shape:** low priority. If ever: the tech_noir-style score
  with sectional hard cuts — synthesized "samples" (short chopped
  chords, guitar-like noise bursts), never real ones.

### All Tomorrow's Parties — 5:05 · ≈95 BPM (tags 95/96; a 191 read is a doubling; a 4:18 edit tags 119) · "A"
The Velvet Underground cover (Lou Reed). Keeps "the basic pattern of
the original but totally reworking the instrumentation" — it "honors
the spirit of the original rather than mocks it". Groth named the
Velvets first among his influences.
- **Mechanism to capture:** the genre translation — a hypnotic,
  repetitive 60s pattern re-voiced on SH-101/Juno/EPS. Proof that
  the palette can carry a *slow* song.
- **Repo shape:** the slow one at ~95; a template for how a borrowed
  pattern becomes EBM by voicing alone (relevant to the
  classical-source list in `tracks/trance/idea.md`).

### Ashes to Ashes '93 — 4:18 · 122 BPM · A minor (detected)
The 1991 debut single re-recorded: "the full-throttle slam it always
was" with better recording. The song exists in more versions than any
other Apop track — 12" (4:08, 1991), *German Slam Version* (3:51),
*Guitar Version* (3:25, Odden), *4-Track Version* (3:59, the demo).
Title and theme sit inside the album's liturgical frame.
- **Mechanism to capture:** THE Apop slam — 122, A minor, kick and
  snare hitting like one instrument, a bass in gated 8ths, a shouted/
  sung chorus, the whole thing short and relentless. This is the
  most "classic" archetype on the record and the safest single
  target.
- **Repo shape:** the first track to build (§6): 122 BPM, A minor,
  seed 1991.

## 4. The interludes — and the album as one piece

Five short pieces (1:12–1:53) frame and separate the songs; the
listener's guide credits them with making the album "work as a
complete piece."

| # | title | length | detected | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Like Blood From The Beloved (Part 1) | 1:31 | ≈113, A♯ minor | opens the record; Wøllo co-credited on the reissue metadata |
| 5 | Walk With Me | 1:40 | — | between *Stitch* and *Backdraft* — the breath before the jackhammer |
| 7 | Arp (808 Edit) | 2:08 | — | "a brief analogue synth indulgence"; the 1992 home recording; the 808 drums are the "edit"; Groth once played the vinyl at 45 instead of 33 and liked it (the Beranek remix came from that) — the piece survives +50 % speed, i.e. it is *pure sequence* |
| 11 | The Sentinel | 1:12 | — | the shortest; later reworked by Blackhouse (a Christian industrial act — the frame again) |
| 13 | Like Blood From The Beloved (Part 2) | 1:53 | ≈115, D♯ minor | closes the record: the opener's idea returned |

**The form lesson:** *Part 1 / Part 2* is a **bookend** — the same idea
opens and closes the album, which is exactly the thesis/bookend device
of the song doctrine (`tracks/trance/idea.md`), at album scale. The
interludes are **seams**: every hard stylistic jump on the record
(*Stitch* → *Backdraft*, *Backdraft* → *Spiritual Reality*, *All
Tomorrow's Parties* → *Ashes to Ashes*) is crossed by a short
instrumental. Sequencing by tempo:

```
113 → 120 → 129 → slow → (int) → 147 → (int) → 109 → suite → 95 → (int) → 122 → 115
 P1   Bitch  Heretic Stitch WWM  Backdr  Arp   SpirR  Sky    ATP   Sent  Ashes  P2
```

Front-loaded singles, the peak at *Backdraft* in the middle, a slower
second half, one last slam, the bookend. **Three tempo archetypes, not
one club tempo:** the 120–129 stomper, the 147 jackhammer, the 95–109
hammer/ballad. A "1993 Apop" generation effort should pick one
archetype per track and keep the interlude/bookend habit.

## 5. The sound signature — ten things that ARE Soli Deo Gloria

1. **The SH-101 bass as engine.** Gated 8ths (stompers, hammer) or
   the gallop (jackhammer); filter-envelope pluck; a little resonance;
   root-pedal verses; mono, dry, centre.
2. **Sampled, truncated, mono drums.** Hard kick, a snare that
   *slams*, no tails, 13-bit grain. "Hammering", "jackhammering",
   "slam" — every reviewer reaches for the same word: percussion as
   blows.
3. **Juno-60 goth colour.** Chorused minor pads in thin voicings,
   offbeat chorused stabs, the arpeggiator on the interludes.
4. **Sampler crunch as texture.** Aliasing, abrupt sample ends,
   chopped orchestral/choir hits, grainy speech snippets — the lo-fi
   is the polish.
5. **Two vocal modes.** "Doom-laden" (clean, low, solemn) and
   "scary-evil" (harsh, distorted, rhythmic) — often within one song
   (verse bark, chorus sung). The instrumental translation needs
   *both* voices: a clean refrain carrier and a distorted bark.
6. **A catchy chorus over the machine.** The Depeche gene — the hook
   is sung and hummable even when the verse is a bark. Choruses are
   short and repeated; the title is usually the hook.
7. **Three tempos.** 120–129 / 147 / 95–109. Not one grid.
8. **Interludes and a bookend.** Album-as-piece; short instrumental
   seams; the opener's idea returns at the end.
9. **One rock texture.** A single thrashy guitar riff on one track —
   Norwegian 1993, the metal scene next door — used as a stab, never
   as the lead.
10. **The religious frame.** Not in the sound directly, but in the
    *tone*: solemn, liturgical, life-and-death; choir/organ-flavoured
    hits expected on *Spiritual Reality* / *The Sentinel* (verify).

**What it is NOT** (keep these out of a 1993-flavoured track):
trance arps walls, strings sections, supersaws, sidechain pump,
sub-bass layers, long hall on everything, swing, humanised drums,
major keys, a tenor futurepop hook — all of that is *7* (1996) and
*Welcome to Earth* (2000), covered in `EBM_1990s.md`.

## 6. Translation to this repo

**Where it lives:** the same `tracks/ebm/` directory proposed in
`EBM_1990s.md`; this is its *dark-electro* dialect. Standalone scripts
per `tracks/dune/CLAUDE.md`; printed verify per `tracks/VERIFY.md`;
FLAC + WAV to `/workspace/music/`; English titles; instrumental only.
Seeds: **1991** (the *Ashes to Ashes* single), **1993**, **18** (TATCD
018).

**The 1993 kit** (status per the instrument catalog in
`tracks/trance/instruments/README.md`):

| element | repo source | status | the 1993 version |
|---|---|---|---|
| kick | `make_kick` (909 family) | free | + `tanh(1.8)`, LP 5 kHz, ×3 zero-order hold (sampler grain), hard cut at 120 ms (truncated sample) |
| slam snare | — | **new** (`eps_snare`) | 200 Hz body + 1.5–7 kHz noise, gated plate cut at 140 ms, ZOH dirt; on 2&4 continuously (tech_noir's slam is punctuation on 13/16 — different contract) |
| hats | canonical | free | closed 16ths, mono, no open hat in verses; hats are quiet in this dialect |
| 808 kit (interludes) | — | **new** (`k808`, `s808`, `h808`) | 55 Hz long sine kick, noise+180 Hz snare, square-cluster hats; interludes and *Arp* only |
| SH-101 bass | `lost_v6:bass_note` voicing | **new** (`sh101_note`) | ONE saw + sub-octave square 0.4; filter env 1600→300 Hz in 90 ms; Q 2.5 blend 0.4 (declared exception, guardrails: tanh 1.0, sine body, envelope always moving); gate 0.5 of the step; ZOH ×2 |
| Juno pad | `pad_chord` | **new** (`juno_pad`) | saw + sub square, chorus = two copies delayed 8/11 ms with ±0.3 % LFO detune, LP 1200, thin 2–3-note voicings, no width tricks beyond the chorus |
| Juno stab | `nachtkind:make_stab` (owned) | **new** (`juno_stab`) | the pad voice gated 60 ms, offbeat, chorused; differs from the nachtkind stab by the chorus + no 900 Hz lid |
| Juno arp | `lost_v6:pluck` | free (re-voiced) | plain saw, arpeggiator up/down 1–2 oct, 16ths or 8ths, dry; the *Arp* piece |
| orchestral/choir hit | — | **new** (`eps_hit`) | saw-stack chord + formant choir + noise burst, 250 ms, hard truncated, ZOH ×3; sparse punctuation |
| refrain voice (clean) | warm-lead family, sustained | **new** (`chorus_voice`, shared with the futurepop plan) | Groth's 1993 register is lower/darker than his 2000 tenor — measure it (§7) before fixing the range |
| the bark (harsh voice slot) | — | **new** (`bark`) | gated saw, two formant peaks (Q 6), tanh(2.0), 16th-rhythmic, pitched to the root/♭3; texture, never melody |
| guitar riff (optional) | — | **new**, declared per track | saw-stack power chord E2–E3, palm-mute gate 80 ms, tanh 1.6, LP 3 kHz; one track only |
| noise/drone bed (*Stitch*) | air bed (in-line) | free | 60–400 Hz brown-noise seethe + a slow LP sweep |
| master | big-room chain | default, **lighter** | no sub-boom layer (the 101 + kick are the low end), duck ≤ 0.35 on pads only, HP 30 Hz, keep the tanh glue; the lo-fi comes from the *instruments*, never from degrading the bus |

**Banned lifts** (as in `EBM_1990s.md`) plus, for this dialect: no
arps wall, no strings, no supersaw, no sub-boom — anything that would
turn 1993 into 2000.

**Three track archetypes, one each:**

| archetype | model | BPM | key | engine | drums |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the slam | *Ashes to Ashes '93*, *Bitch* | 120–122 | A minor / C♯ minor | SH-101 gated 8ths on a pedal; chorus follows i–VI–VII | kick every beat, slam 2&4 |
| the jackhammer | *Backdraft* | 147 | C♯ minor | gallop cell, 8ths on the 101 | double-kick figures, snare runs, 16th hats |
| the hammer | *Spiritual Reality* | ~109 | F♯ minor | mechanical 8ths, root only | huge dry snare, slow, heavy |

Plus the **interlude/bookend device**: a 1:30 arpeggiated *Part 1*
that opens the track (thesis) and a *Part 2* that closes it — the
album's habit compressed into one track satisfies the doctrine's
thesis/bookend rule natively. Start with **the slam**: 122 BPM, A
minor, seed 1991, ~4:20, hook target ≥ 8, the bark in the verses and
the clean refrain in the choruses, one Juno stab on the offbeat, an
808 *Part 1/Part 2* frame.

**Verify block additions** (on top of the standard set and the EBM
checks in `EBM_1990s.md` §12): the **truncation check** (every drum
sample's length ≤ its declared cut, printed), the **bass gate duty**
(≤ 0.5 of the step — stricter than the futurepop 0.6; this bass
stomps), the **two-voice separation** (bark activity in verses ≫
choruses; refrain voice the reverse — printed overlap ratio), and the
**bookend match** (Part 2 restates Part 1's arpeggio cell — checked
from the note lists).

## 7. Calibration — the inspector runs that settle the open numbers

No audio was measured. When the 1993 CD or the 2018 remaster is
available under `/workspace/music/refs/sdg/` (`yt-dlp` is not
installed here; files only), run from `/repos/music`:

```bash
# every track: BPM stability, key, structure
for f in /workspace/music/refs/sdg/*.flac; do
  n=$(basename "$f" .flac)
  python3 -m inspector.analyse "$f" --plots --out "inspiration/sdg_refs/$n.md"
done

# the three archetypes, one chorus each: stems for the bass cell, the drums, the vocal register
python3 -m inspector.analyse /workspace/music/refs/sdg/12_ashes_to_ashes_93.flac --start 60 --end 90 --separate \
    --stems-out /workspace/music/refs/sdg/ashes_stems --out inspiration/sdg_refs/ashes_chorus.md
python3 -m inspector.analyse /workspace/music/refs/sdg/06_backdraft.flac --start 60 --end 90 --separate \
    --stems-out /workspace/music/refs/sdg/backdraft_stems --out inspiration/sdg_refs/backdraft_chorus.md
```

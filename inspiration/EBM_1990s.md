# 1990s EBM → Futurepop — the Apoptygma Berzerk / VNV Nation blueprint

*A genre blueprint for generation (not a single-track analysis, unlike
the other docs in this directory). Written 2026-09-05 from genre
knowledge; **no reference audio was measured** — the BPM/key figures are
ranges from memory, and §13 says exactly which inspector runs fill them
in once reference files exist. Blueprint → `*_notes.md` with open
questions → script, as always.*

---

## 0. What "1990s EBM" means here — the three dialects

"EBM" spans twenty years and three quite different sounds. The bands the
user named sit at the *hinge* between the second and third:

| dialect | years | bands | sound | tempo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Old-school EBM** | 1981–1991 | DAF, Front 242, Nitzer Ebb, The Klinik, A Split-Second, Die Krupps | Riff-based, no chords, barked vocals, dry 12-bit crunch, one sequenced bass as the whole engine | 115–135 |
| **Dark electro / electro-industrial** | 1991–1997 | Front Line Assembly, Leæther Strip, :wumpscut:, Project Pitchfork, **Apop *Soli Deo Gloria* (1993) and *7* (1996)**, VNV *Advance and Conquer* (1995) | EBM bass kept, choruses arrive, film samples everywhere, distortion + long reverb coexist | 120–140 |
| **Futurepop** | 1998–2003 | **VNV *Praise the Fallen* (1998), *Empires* (1999)**, **Apop *Welcome to Earth* (2000)**, Covenant, Icon of Coil, Assemblage 23, Neuroticfish | Trance production (arps, unison saws, big strings) under a sung pop chorus on top of an EBM stomp | 128–145 |

**Target for generation: the hinge, 1996–2000** — *7* and *Empires*.
That is the point where the EBM bass engine still runs the track but a
hummable chorus is the destination. The word "futurepop" was coined by
Ronan Harris (VNV) and Stephan Groth (Apop) themselves for exactly this
mix: EBM body + synthpop song + trance synths.

What stays constant across all three dialects — the DNA:

1. **The body.** Straight 4/4, kick on every quarter, a real SNARE on 2
   and 4 (not a clap — that is the single fastest tell against trance).
2. **The sequenced bass is the engine.** Gated, staccato, monophonic,
   on a 16th grid, more rhythm than melody. Everything else is decoration.
3. **Minor key, always.** Aeolian; harmonic-minor V only for drama.
4. **Machine precision, zero swing.** Hardware-sequencer stiffness is
   the aesthetic, not a limitation to hide.
5. **One organic element: the human voice.** Everything else is
   synthetic. (In this repo the voice slot is filled instrumentally — §8.)

What changes along the timeline: chords (none → four), chorus (none →
everything), polish (12-bit sampler grit → JP-8000/Virus gloss), space
(bone dry → cathedral hall on everything but the drums).

## 1. Genre & Style

- **Primary:** late-90s EBM/futurepop — "sad songs you can stomp to".
- **Apoptygma Berzerk (Norway, Stephan Groth):** the melodic pole. Clean
  tenor, boyish, doubled and reverbed; catchy pop choruses; bright
  trance-adjacent hooks; bittersweet rather than dark. *7* is the
  bridge album (EBM bass + sample intros + pop chorus); *Welcome to
  Earth* is full futurepop (unison-saw leads, arps, club mixes).
- **VNV Nation (Ronan Harris, Irish, London → Hamburg):** the solemn
  pole. Baritone, declamatory, hymn-like; lyrics of fate, honour,
  defiance, humanity; orchestral colours (strings, choir, timpani,
  cathedral-scale pads); instrumental overtures/codas on the albums
  (*Firstlight* opens *Empires*, *Arclight* closes it). "Victory Not
  Vengeance."
- **Peers on the same hinge:** Covenant (Sweden — colder, more
  minimal, the best basslines of the three), Icon of Coil (Norway,
  Apop-adjacent), Assemblage 23 (US — the songwriting), And One
  (Germany — the retro-EBM humour), Wolfsheim (the synthpop border).
- **Grandparents:** Depeche Mode for the songcraft (*Violator*-era),
  Front 242 for the bass, Kraftwerk for the stiffness.

## 2. Tempo & Key

- **Tempo:** 128–140 BPM for the hinge era. Apop *7*-era sits ~125–135,
  *Welcome to Earth* ~135–140; VNV *Empires* ~130–140; old-school EBM
  slower (Front 242 / Nitzer Ebb ~120–132); ballads (Apop "Mourn",
  VNV "Beloved"-type) drop to ~100–128. **Unverified — see §13.**
- **Safe generation spec:** **136 BPM** — off every existing track's
  tempo in this repo (130/132/138/139/142/145/147) and inside both
  bands' range.
- **Key:** natural minor throughout. A, E, D, G, F#, C# minor all
  common. The harmonic-minor raised 7th (the V-major chord) appears at
  the pre-chorus → chorus hinge in the VNV school; Apop stays Aeolian.
- **Practical note:** the bass sits on the root of each chord in
  choruses (it *follows* the progression bar by bar — the futurepop
  tell) and on a tonic pedal in verses (the EBM tell). Both in one
  song.

## 3. Song Structure

Futurepop is pop song form, 4:30–6:00 on the album, 16-bar blocks:

| bars | section | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–16 | **Intro** | Pad + arp or a sample; kick enters halfway (bar 9) or at the verse. Often the chorus melody is *quoted* here on a piano/lead — the thesis, era-authentic. |
| 17–32 | **Verse 1** | Kick + snare + EBM bass on a pedal; vocal low and narrow; one arp or pluck; pad thin. |
| 33–40 | **Pre-chorus** | Bass starts following chords; melody rises; hats open; snare roll on the last 2 bars + a crash. |
| 41–56 | **Chorus 1** | Everything: hook sung twice (8+8), strings/pad full, arp doubled, bass on chord roots, snare + clap layered. |
| 57–72 | **Verse 2** | Back to the pedal but one element denser than verse 1 (a counter-arp, a hat carpet). |
| 73–80 | **Pre-chorus 2** | As before, one more layer. |
| 81–96 | **Chorus 2** | Identical hook; may add the octave-up lead doubling. |
| 97–112 | **Bridge / breakdown** | Kick OUT. Strings/choir/piano carry the harmony; the film-sample slot; the vocal's oldest question. Last 4 bars: roll + riser + the one silent beat. |
| 113–144 | **Final chorus ×2** | The double chorus: first pass as chorus 2, second pass with the counter-melody (the fusion); the loudest section. |
| 145–160 | **Outro** | Chorus instrumental with arp, then strip to bass + kick, or a hard stop on the last downbeat. |

**Old-school EBM form** (for reference, not the target): intro riff →
riff + drums → shouted verse → riff break → verse → riff out. No chorus,
no chords; sections change by muting/unmuting layers, exactly like the
Dominator blueprint.

**Mapping onto the song doctrine** (`tracks/trance/idea.md`): this IS
song form — refrain identity, verse asks / chorus answers, bridge asks
the oldest question, final chorus fuses. The only friction is the
16-bar arp intro vs the thesis-early / groove-first rule; §12 resolves
it (thesis quoted in an 8-bar intro, kick at bar 9).

## 4. Melody & Harmony

- **Progressions — one family per track**, four chords, one bar each
  (or two-bar in verses), looped:
  - **i–VI–III–VII** (Am–F–C–G) — the futurepop anthem loop; the
    "sad-triumphant" one. Apop's home.
  - **i–VI–VII–i** / **i–VII–VI–VII** (Am–F–G–Am / Am–G–F–G) — the
    darker, more EBM loop; VNV verses.
  - **i–iv–VI–V** (Am–Dm–F–E) — the VNV chorus with the raised 7th
    (G#) as the one dramatic colour; the pre-chorus parks on V.
  - **VI–VII–i–i** (F–G–Am–Am) — the chorus that *lands* on the tonic
    on bar 3 (the lift feels like arrival, not departure).
  - Verses frequently reduce the loop to a **tonic pedal** (i only) —
    the harmony is the reward of the chorus.
- **The vocal melody IS the refrain.** Verse melody: narrow (a 5th),
  low, syllabic, repeated-note recitation on the root and ♭3. Chorus
  melody: wide (an octave), high, long notes on chord tones, the
  climax on the ♭6 or the 5th above the tonic, resolving down to the
  root on the last chord. Phrase shape: two 4-bar phrases, question
  (ends on 5th or ♭7) / answer (ends on root) — the doctrine's Q/A.
- **Register matters** (this is the design lever for an instrumental
  version): Groth is a **tenor** — hooks live ~A3–A4, peaking C5;
  Harris is a **baritone** — hooks live ~A2–E4. A synth "singer" in
  the baritone register with a formant bump reads unmistakably as a
  voice-shaped line; in the tenor register it reads as a trance lead.
- **Counter-melody:** the arp *is* the counter-melody (chord tones,
  16ths); a second sustained line (strings/lead an octave up) joins
  the final chorus only — the earned fusion.
- **No key changes, no modulation, no Picardy** (the goa lesson
  applies: major = country). A single harmonic family, re-lit by
  density.

## 5. Bassline — THE instrument

The EBM bass is a **gated, staccato, monophonic 16th-grid sequence**.
Rhythm first, pitch second. Think SH-101 / MC-202 / Pro-One driven by a
hardware sequencer: every note the same length, dead-stopped, no
legato, no slides. **It is not a 303** — no resonance scream, no
portamento, no ties; the 303 family is claimed by silver_wire /
morgenland / flightpath anyway.

**Cells** (16 steps per bar; `x` = root, `o` = root +12, `5` = fifth,
`7` = ♭7, `.` = rest; K = kick position):

```
                 K...K...K...K...
stomp (8ths)     x.x.x.x.x.x.x.x.    old-school; root only, octave on the "&" of 4
gallop           x.xxx.xxx.xxx.xx    the Front 242 school (steps 0,2,3 per beat)
offbeat          .x.x.x.x.x.x.x.x    the futurepop/trance off-8th; bass NEVER on the kick
rolling+octave   xoxxxoxxxoxxxoxx    the VNV chorus engine (root–oct–root–root)
riff (DAF)       x..x..x...x.5...    syncopated, pentatonic; the barked-vocal bed
answer           x.x.x.x.7.7.o.5.    last bar of a 4-bar cell: the turnaround
```

- **Verse:** stomp or gallop on the tonic pedal, 60–250 Hz, dark.
- **Pre-chorus:** offbeat cell, bass starts moving to chord roots.
- **Chorus:** rolling+octave following the progression (root of each
  chord, bar by bar); the octave note is the brightness.
- **Gate:** note length **50–60 % of a 16th step**; the gap *is* the
  groove. Never continuous 16ths that blur — that's rolling trance
  bass (canonical `lost_v6:bass_note`, whose *voicing* is the right
  starting point but whose continuous legato is the wrong gait).
- **Voicing:** saw (or saw+square) rolled `1/k**1.3`, lowpass with a
  **filter envelope**: cutoff opens to ~1400–1800 Hz at the attack and
  decays to ~300 Hz within 80–120 ms (the pluck-thump). Resonance
  **Q ≈ 2.0, blend 0.4** — a sanctioned exception to the warmth
  recipe, with its three guardrails: soft drive `tanh(1.0)`, a sine
  sub at 0.35 for body, and the cutoff always *moving* (the envelope)
  — never parked. The bite lives in the 300–900 Hz pluck, not in a
  parked 2–4 kHz whistle. HP at 35 Hz.
- **Old-school variant:** more drive `tanh(1.6)`, sampler dirt (×3
  zero-order hold, as adrift's piano — a technique, declare it),
  lowpass 4 kHz, mono, centre, bone dry.
- **Verify:** printed cell per section, gate duty per cell (≤ 0.6),
  onset count per bar matches the declared cell, "bass never on the
  kick 16th" for the offbeat cell, and a **root ledger** in choruses
  (bass root per bar == chord root per bar).

## 6. Drums & Percussion

- **Kick:** 909 family, four-on-the-floor, **harder than trance**:
  canonical `make_kick` (48+102·e^(−50t)) through `tanh(1.6)` with a
  short click, plus the master's sub-boom for weight. Old-school: a
  sampled-kick crunch (lowpass 5 kHz + ZOH dirt).
- **Snare on 2 and 4 — the genre's fingerprint.** Not a clap. A
  190–220 Hz body sine burst + 1.5–7 kHz noise, ~180 ms, with a **short
  gated plate** (dense tail cut dead at ~140 ms). Continuous in every
  groove section (this separates it from tech_noir's slam, which is
  punctuation on a 13/16 grid — a new instrument, declared). In
  choruses layer the canonical clap on top for width.
- **Hats:** closed 16th carpet with a per-16th accent cell
  (`1.0 .5 .7 .5`), open hat on the off-8ths in pre-choruses and
  choruses only; a ride enters in the final chorus (era discipline).
- **Toms:** 909 toms as fills at the three seams (verse→chorus roll
  substitute, into the bridge, out of the bridge) — the ≤3 rule.
  Old-school adds a low tom doubling beat 3 (the Nitzer Ebb tribal
  stomp).
- **Rolls:** the snare roll (8th → 16th → 32nd) + bandlimited swell
  into every chorus — canonical `roll` + `swell`. Crash on every
  chorus downbeat.
- **Metal:** old-school EBM has clanks and pipe hits; futurepop
  doesn't. Skip (tech_noir owns anvil/tap anyway).
- **Feel:** rigid, quantised, no swing, no ghost notes on the snare.
  Velocity is a step pattern, not a humanisation.

## 7. Synths & Sound Design

- **Arpeggio (futurepop signature):** 16th-note chord-tone arp, 1–2
  octaves, up or up-down, glassy pluck (canonical `lost_v6:pluck`,
  STEP×3 decay), dotted-8th ping-pong delay. Cutoff per *section*
  (table), never a track-long ramp — the anti-arc rule holds here
  too. Two arps in the final chorus (one an octave up, offset by a
  16th) is the VNV wall.
- **Lead:** unison detuned saws — the JP-8000 supersaw is era-correct
  from 1997, so ±0.4 % ×3 (canonical warm lead) up to ×5 detuned
  copies is allowed; lowpass 2800–3200 Hz, chorus width, rounded
  attack. The warmth guardrails still apply (rolled partials, sine
  body, soft drive). Apop's leads are *bright*; keep the brightness in
  the detune and the octave doubling, not in a raw `1/k` spectrum.
- **The chorus voice (the vocal slot — §8):** a sustained voice with a
  slow-ish attack, bloom vibrato, and a formant bump — in the
  singer's register. Sustain + slow attack + vibrato is the standing
  law for any refrain carrier (the eisgang lesson).
- **Strings:** big synth strings, JV-1080/Trinity rompler character
  — in the repo, `ungeschrieben:strings_line/strings_chord` are the
  reference but are *owned*; a new "JV strings" voice differs by 4
  detuned saws, a slower 0.3 s attack, no sample-loop flutter, a
  brighter bow layer. Chorus + bridge only.
- **Choir / cathedral pad (VNV):** wordless, formant pair (650/1080)
  on a rolled saw, octave-doubled, long hall. adrift owns the breath
  choir; the "cathedral choir" is distinct by being *chordal and
  static* (no drifting voices, no 8 % breath) — declare it.
- **Piano (Apop):** the hook instrument on "Kathy's Song"/"Love Never
  Dies" is a bright, dry-ish rompler grand — NOT nachtkind's gothic M1
  piano (owned, and too wet/funereal). A new voice: same stretched
  partials, `1/k**1.15` (brighter), hammer at 0.22, short room only.
- **Pads:** the canonical dark sine pad for verses; the strings for
  choruses. Wide (±0.12 %, cross 0.40 — the big-room default).
- **FX:** white-noise risers are **era-correct here** (1998+) — lost's
  `riser` is free; reverse cymbals are nachtkind's (use the roll +
  swell instead); a downsweep after the chorus crash.
- **Layering logic:** never more than kick / snare / hats / bass / arp
  / one pad / one lead at a time in verses; choruses add strings +
  second arp + doubled lead. Density is the arc.

## 8. Vocals & Samples — and how the repo fills the slot

**What the records do:**

- **Groth (Apop):** clean melodic tenor, doubled, plate + dotted-8th
  delay on tails, Norwegian-accented English, sung *sweetly* over a
  hard beat — the contrast is the hook.
- **Harris (VNV):** baritone, chest voice, declamatory, near-spoken in
  verses, opening into long held notes in choruses; hymn diction;
  minimal vibrato; long hall.
- **Old-school:** barked, shouted, monotone, often distorted or
  delayed into a rhythm; German or English; military imagery.
- **Samples:** film dialogue in intros and bridges (sci-fi, religion,
  war; Blade Runner-class), stuttered/retriggered in old-school and
  dark-electro; futurepop keeps one clean spoken drop per song.

**What this repo does (standing rules, 2026-07-10 and the unsung
verdict):** instrumental only — the vocal slot is carried by an
instrument, never TTS speech or singing; no spoken-word drops.
So:

- The **refrain** is played by the chorus voice (§7) in the singer's
  register: **baritone A2–E4 for the VNV colour, tenor A3–C5 for the
  Apop colour** — pick one per track, print the median pitch of the
  refrain and check it sits inside the declared range.
- The refrain **sings**: 2–4 notes/s, held phrase endings, breath
  rests between phrases, ornaments only on held notes (the
  sung-grammar checks from morgenland v2: density window 0.20–0.50,
  run ceiling ≤ 6, held fraction ≥ 0.5).
- Verse "vocal": the same voice, lower, narrower, recitation on ♭3/root
  — or silent, letting the bass and arp be the verse.
- The **sample slot** in the bridge is filled by an instrumental
  statement: the piano/organ stating the hook alone, or the bass
  riff naked on the pedal. Never a voice.

## 9. Effects & Production

- **Reverb split:** drums dry-to-short (the gated snare plate is the
  only drum reverb), bass bone dry and centred; vocals/leads/strings/
  choir in a long hall (4–6 s, wet 0.4–0.55). The dry engine / wet
  heart split — the same contrast as Frankfurt trance.
- **Delay:** dotted-8th ping-pong on the arp and the chorus voice's
  tails; quarter-note feedback delay on the bridge strings.
- **Distortion:** on the kick (tanh 1.6) and the bass (tanh 1.0;
  old-school 1.6); nothing else. The mix is *clean* in 1999 — the
  grit is old-school only.
- **Compression / loudness:** firm but not brickwalled (1996–2000 —
  pre-loudness-war); no sidechain pump on the records. In the repo
  the big-room master is the validated default — use it with a
  **lighter duck (0.35–0.45 instead of 0.55)** because the bass is
  already gated and *is* the rhythm; pump on pads/strings only, never
  the chorus voice or the bass. Print sub shares and crest per
  section per the master guardrails (HP 30 Hz, crest ≥ 3.2 in loud
  sections).
- **Filtering:** per-section cutoff tables on the bass and arp
  (darker verses, open choruses); a high-pass on everything but bass
  and kick in the bridge (the classic "the floor drops out" sound).
- **Builds:** snare roll + swell + noise riser into every chorus; the
  one composed silent beat before the final double chorus; a crash on
  every chorus downbeat.
- **Stereo:** kick/snare/bass mono-centre; arps and delays wide;
  strings widest; the chorus voice centre with a wide double in
  choruses only.

## 10. Emotional Tone & Energy Arc

- **Mood:** melancholic-triumphant. Apop: bittersweet, romantic,
  boyish defiance. VNV: solemn, martial dignity, grief turned into
  resolve — the hymn in a nightclub. Old-school: aggression,
  discipline, the body as a machine.
- **Arc:** simmer (verse, pedal, low voice) → lift (pre-chorus,
  chords start moving, roll) → **explosion (chorus: everything, the
  hook)** → reset one notch higher (verse 2) → second explosion →
  **the trough (bridge: kick out, strings, the oldest question)** →
  silent beat → **the double final chorus with the fusion** → outro
  settle or hard stop.
- **Shape summary:** classic pop valley-and-peak with the chorus as
  the destination every time — the opposite of the hypnotic plateau
  (Dominator) and the single wave (Symmetry). Repetition is *of the
  hook*, not of a loop.
- **Descriptors:** anthemic, defiant, melancholic, stomping,
  cathedral-scale, machine-precise, sung-over-steel.

**Banned lifts by construction** (identity separation): the entire 303
family (no acid, no slides, no iirpeak scream), the gothic piano,
reverse cymbal, slam/anvil/tap, thud-bass/tick pair/stamp, the psy kit
and K-b-b-b, all Vivaldi W-mechanisms, rompler-string flutter, the
breath choir's drift. EBM's bass must not turn into rolling trance
bass (gate duty is the check) and the chorus voice must not turn into
a percussive pling (sustain is the check).

**Declared exceptions to argue in the notes doc:** (1) bass resonance
Q 2.0 / blend 0.4 with guardrails; (2) the snare as a continuous
groove element with a gated plate (the only reverb on a drum); (3) the
8-bar intro that quotes the hook on piano/arp over the pad, kick at
bar 9 — satisfies both the futurepop arp-intro convention and the
thesis-early / groove-first rule; (4) a lighter master duck.

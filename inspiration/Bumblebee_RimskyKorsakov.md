# Musical analysis of "Flight of the Bumblebee" (Полёт шмеля) — Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov

Orchestral interlude closing Act III, Tableau 1 of *The Tale of Tsar
Saltan* (composed 1899–1900, premiered 3 November 1900 at the
Solodovnikov Theatre, Moscow; libretto by Vladimir Belsky after
Pushkin's 1831 fairy-tale poem). Dramatic context: the Swan-Bird
transforms Prince Gvidon into a bumblebee so he can fly across the sea
and secretly see his father, the Tsar who does not know he exists; the
buzz music returns in the next tableau when the bee stings the
conspirators — the two aunts and old Babarikha.

Why this document exists: **this is the ur-run piece.** The Silver Wire
v2 verdict confirmed cell-built running lines as the 303-melody recipe
(density 0.90, 112-step run, accent cycles, slide chains). Bumblebee is
that grammar written in 1900 — melodic density ~1.0 sustained for two
minutes, built from about eight one-bar cells, and it stays a *piece*
instead of an exercise. This is a grammar audit, not a cover blueprint:
what keeps a maximum-density run listenable, and which of its engines
we can lift without triggering the gag (see wear status).

**Verification**: every pitch below was read programmatically from a
MIDI of the standard violin/piano concert edition
(`Bumblebee_score/bumble_bee.mid`, 8notes.com rendering of the
public-domain score) — 1,193 melody notes and all 152 bars parsed, the
interval histogram and structure claims computed, not eyeballed. This
is the concert reduction, not the full opera score; orchestration notes
in §7 are from general knowledge of the orchestral version.

## 1. Genre & Style

Orchestral scherzo-interlude, perpetuum mobile, programme music at its
most literal: the melody does not *depict* the bee, it **is** the bee —
pitch contour as flight path. Monothematic and cell-built: one buzz
sentence, a handful of chromatic cells, a transposition plan, and
register as the only dramatic axis. No counterpoint to speak of, no
second subject — the interest is 100% in the run grammar and where the
run *sits*.

**Wear status, honestly: this is the most-worn virtuoso gag in
classical music.** The Green Hornet theme, Rachmaninoff's piano
transcription, Harry James's swing version, every shred guitarist's
party trick — the *surface* (fast chromatic wiggle on a lead
instrument, especially the opening plunge) is radioactive; any literal
statement reads as a joke within two bars. But nobody thinks of
Bumblebee as a *form*. The anchoring system, the trill duel, the
sink-and-ramp, the wedges, the register coda — unmined. Same verdict
pattern as Winter: leave the claimed surface, take the engines.

## 2. Tempo & Key

- **Vivace, ♩ = 144, 2/4** — 152 bars ≈ 2:07 in this edition; concert
  performances push to ~1:05–1:30 by raising tempo; the opera version
  runs longer with the Swan-Bird's vocal.
- **Key**: the opera original centers on **A minor** (buzz anchored on
  E; Wikipedia lists B minor and F♯ minor waypoints). The verified
  concert edition sits in **D minor** (buzz anchored on A) and
  organizes its wandering as a chain of fourths: **Dm → Gm → Cm → A
  pedal → Dm**. All pitches below are the D minor edition's; transpose
  freely — the grammar is interval-relative anyway.

**The number that matters: 16ths at ♩144 = 9.6 notes/sec. Trance 16ths
at 138–144 BPM = 9.2–9.6 notes/sec.** The bee already flies at exactly
trance velocity — a 303 running 16ths is a bumblebee by construction.
No tempo translation needed at all; a Bumblebee cell dropped onto a
138 BPM grid plays at the speed Rimsky-Korsakov marked.

## 3. Song Structure

Form is **A A′ + coda** — the recap (b59–110) restates b7–58
*verbatim*; all development after that is register and doubling, never
new material. Zero silent bars; every section boundary is crossed by a
climb bar or a plunge (the doctrine's "no dead seams," 126 years
early).

- **b1–6 — The plunge (B1)**: three-octave chromatic fall from A6 on a
  single struck A-major chord (pure dominant).
- **b7–14 — The buzz sentence (B2)**, D minor, anchored on A.
- **b15–22 — Sentence up a 4th (B3)**, G minor, anchored on D.
- **b23–30 — Hammer-and-trill duel (B4)** on D, exits through an
  augmented dominant.
- **b31–38 — Duel up a 4th** on G, again exiting through an augmented
  dominant.
- **b39–42 — C minor episode**: the buzz cells developed in ♭VII minor,
  with chromatic slippage.
- **b43–44 — Plunge recap**, landing on the dominant.
- **b45–58 — The sink and the ramp (B5)**: register trough on a bare A
  pedal, then a written-out two-octave chromatic riser.
- **b59–110 — Full literal recap** of b7–58.
- **b111–126 — Coda I**: the sentence and the Gm block restated 1–2
  octaves up over full chords and a high D6 pedal; climax chords
  b124–125; scalar launch bar (b126) to the top.
- **b127–138 — Coda II, the stratosphere (B6)**: theme at A6 on the
  violin's E-string ceiling; 4-bar swell phrase stated high, then
  answered two octaves down while the accompaniment leaps to D7 — a
  composed register swap.
- **b139–142 — The waterfall**: the plunge cells return re-anchored on
  D (the root, at last), falling two octaves, harmonized i → V7/iv → iv
  (the piece's one secondary dominant).
- **b143–146 — Exit wedges**: hammer/trill bars while the accompaniment
  fans outward chromatically in contrary motion.
- **b147–152 — Exit run**: unbroken chromatic ascent A3 → C♯6 (2½
  octaves), land D6, then a final flick up to **D7**. Ends minor, cold,
  upward — the bee simply leaves the frame (tech_noir precedent: ends
  cold).

## 4. Melody & Harmony — the motif catalog

Pitches in scientific notation (C4 = middle C), verified against the
MIDI. First, the numbers that define the piece:

- **Interval content of all 1,192 melodic steps: 90.2% semitones,
  4.0% repeated notes, 2.7% whole steps, 1.5% perfect-4th "flicks",
  everything else < 1%.** The most chromatic famous melody there is.
- **145 of 152 bars carry all eight 16ths; no bar is silent.** The line
  never stops from the first note to bar 151.
- **Downbeat anchors: A ×58, D ×40, G ×18, F ×16** — chord tones only.
  The chromatic buzz is pinned to plain triads at every downbeat; the
  ear tracks the anchors, the semitones are surface.
- **Range: G♯3 → D7** — 3½ octaves; register is the piece's entire
  dramatic budget.

### B1 — The plunge (b1–6)

Two-bar cell, stated three times, each an octave lower, over one struck
A-major chord:

```
b1: A6 G#6 G6 F#6 | G6 F#6 F6 E6     — sawtooth: down 4 chromatic, back up 1, down again
b2: F6 E6 D#6 D6  | C#6 C6 B5 A#5    — straight chromatic drop
b3–4: same, octave lower (A5→A#4)
b5–6: sawtooth bar TWICE (A4…E4, A4…E4) — no drop bar
```

Detail worth stealing: the third statement replaces the drop bar with a
repeat of the sawtooth — **the fall decelerates by circling before it
lands**. Air brakes. Six bars of pure dominant that state the piece's
thesis (register drama + chromatic grammar) before the tonic ever
sounds.

**Trance translation:** a written-out downlifter that doubles as the
intro thesis — three-octave descent on the dominant, braking into the
groove register. Also the model for any seam that needs to *fall* into
a section rather than rise.

### B2 — The buzz cell (b7–14) — THE theme

One-bar cells over D minor, every downbeat on **A — the fifth of the
chord, not the root**:

```
cell a ("dip & flick"):  A G# G F# | F A# A G#   — 4 down, leap +4th, 3 down
cell b ("V-turn"):       A G# G F# | F F# G G#   — 4 down, 4 back up
```

The flick's target is A♯ — the chromatic *upper neighbour* of the
anchor: the leap lands a semitone above home and re-descends. The whole
melodic system is "the chromatic neighbourhood of the anchor" — nothing
more.

The 8-bar sentence: `a b a b | sawtooth climb sawtooth climb′`, where
`climb` = `F F# G G# A A# A G#` (chromatic ascent answering four bars
of descent-shapes) and `climb′` (b14) = `F F# G G# A B C C#` — the
chromatic grid bends diatonic at the last moment to launch the
transposition. Question/answer *inside* an eight-bar loop of semitones.

**Harmony of the sentence — the hidden gem:** `Dm | G | Dm | G |
Dm→Gm/B♭ | Dm/A→A7 | Dm/F→Gm/B♭ | Dm/A→A7`. That G chord has **B
natural: the major IV, Dorian brightness** — the buzz alternates i↔IV
exactly like a classic acid vamp, then closes iv6→V7. A fully chromatic
melody harmonized with four diatonic chords.

**Trance translation:** this is the 303 recipe with the levers
reassigned. Cells a/b are slide-chain material verbatim (slide the
chromatic 4, accent the flick); the anchor-on-downbeat rule is the
Silver Wire accent cycle expressed as *pitch* instead of velocity; the
i↔IV Dorian vamp is already in the acid songbook. Run it at 138 and
it's not a quote — it's a grammar.

### B3 — The transposition machine (b15–22)

The full 8-bar sentence restated a perfect 4th up: anchor D over G
minor, exits through chromatic passing chords onto D7. That's the whole
development plan of the piece: **don't write a second theme, move the
first one up a 4th and re-harmonize the exits.** With B2's sentence and
this rule you have b7–22, b59–74, b111–126 — nearly half the piece from
one loop and one transposition.

### B4 — The hammer-and-trill duel (b23–38)

New texture, traded between soloist and accompaniment — the doctrine's
between-instruments Q/A as an actual duel:

```
b23: D5 drop, then HAMMER D4 ×7 (16ths, one pitch)
b24: D# D D# C# | D# D D# C#        — written-out trill around the pitch
b25: hammer D5 ×8 (octave up; piano echoes the trill notes between hits)
b26: the trill oscillation, octave up
b27–28: D D# D C# ×4                — the trill becomes the line
b29–30: D D# E F | F# F E D#  (×2)  — palindrome swell, up and back
```

Then the whole block a 4th higher on G (b31–38). Harmonic pins: the
hammer sits on Gm, then G *major*; and under each palindrome swell sits
an **augmented dominant** — B♭–D–F♯ resolving to G (b29→31), G–B–D♯
resolving to C minor (b37→39). Rimsky's fingerprint: symmetrical
harmony as lift, committing to no key until it lands. The transition
into each swell (b28) is a **contrary-motion chromatic wedge** — voices
fanning outward by semitone from a unison D (D → C♯/D♯ → C/E → B/F).

**Trance translation:** hammer bar = one-pitch 16th retrigger (snare
roll's melodic cousin); trill bar = two-semitone gate figure; the duel
= trade these between lead and a second voice bar by bar. The augmented
swell is a pre-chorus lift that doesn't tip the key — rare move,
instantly un-generic. The wedge is a riser and downlifter crossfaded in
one gesture.

### B5 — The sink and the ramp (b45–58) — the breakdown, 1900

After a plunge recap lands on the dominant:

```
b45–48: oscillation cell  A A# A G# | A A# A G#  at A4, harmony thinning
b49–50: plunge cell again, dropping an octave
b51–54: same oscillation at A3 — G-string bottom, piano bare octaves (A2/A3)
b55–58: THE RAMP — unbroken chromatic ascent A3 → G#5 (23 semitones in
        3 bars), landing on the A5 oscillation, all over a bare A pedal
```

Register trough → harmonic strip-down to a naked dominant pedal →
written-out two-octave riser → re-entry into the full recap. That is a
trance breakdown-and-build, note for note, with the riser composed in
pitches instead of automation. The recap that follows (b59) is the
"drop": same material, full harmony restored.

### B6 — The register coda (b111–152)

No new pitch material after b110 — the coda is pure arrangement:

- **b111–126**: sentence + Gm block moved up 1–2 octaves, accompaniment
  now full chords with a high D6 pedal; b126 is a scalar launch bar
  (`D6 E6 F6 G6 A6 A#6` — the grid bends diatonic again to travel fast).
- **b127–138**: theme at **A6**; the 4-bar swell phrase stated at the
  top, then *answered two octaves down* while the piano's pedal leaps
  to D7 — a composed register swap (high/low hocket). Harmony a plain
  Dm↔A7 seesaw; all tension is altitude.
- **b139–142 — the waterfall**: B1's sawtooth+drop pair, re-anchored on
  D, falling D6→D♯4, harmonized i → V7/iv → iv.
- **b143–146 — exit wedges**: violin hammers/trills on D4 then A5 while
  the piano fans outward chromatically in contrary motion (upper voice
  climbing A♯–B–C–C♯ against a falling G–F–E under the A pedal),
  crescendo on A7.
- **b147–152 — exit**: chromatic run A3→C♯6 without a break, land D6,
  flick to **D7**. Minor to the last chord.

**Trance translation:** the coda is the arrangement lesson — after the
final breakdown, don't add parts, *relocate* them. Final chorus = same
loop, +12 or +24 on the lead, pads dropped to bare pedal + one voice at
the ceiling. The two-octave call/answer hocket is a composed octave
gate. And the ending: exit on a rising run to a single high staccato
note — a track that vanishes upward instead of fading.

## 5. Bassline

Four behaviours, all liftable: (1) **struck-chord punctuation** —
quarter-note on-beat chords under the buzz (beats 1 and 2, sometimes 1
only), the minimum harmony that keeps the anchors legible; (2)
**pedals** — the A-major plunge chord (b1–6), the bare A octaves under
the trough and ramp (b45–58), the high D6/D7 pedals inverting the
bass function in the coda; (3) **the walking figure** under the trough
oscillation (`D–B♭–D–F` in quarters+8ths, b46/52); (4) **the wedge** —
contrary-motion chromatic lines framing a pedal (b28, b144, b146), the
piece's cadential signature.

## 6. Drums & Percussion

None in the concert edition — but the pulse roles are cast: the
hammered one-pitch bars are the snare-roll analog, the trill bars are
two-step gate patterns, the struck chords are the downbeat kick grid,
and the plunge/waterfall bars are composed fills. The 16th-note line
itself *is* the hi-hat layer — at 9.6 notes/sec the melody supplies the
subdivision a kit would normally provide, which is why the piece needs
no percussion and why a 303 line at this density wants a sparser kit,
not a busier one.

## 7. Synths & Sound Design (the mapping)

| Rimsky | role | trance voice |
|--------|------|--------------|
| violin/flute chromatic run | the bee | 303 running line — slides on chromatic cells, accent on flicks |
| struck quarter chords | harmonic anchor | offbeat/downbeat stab, dark and short |
| bare-octave dominant pedal (B5) | breakdown floor | sub drone on the fifth, everything else out |
| hammered one-pitch bars | roll/retrigger | one-note 16th retrigger with rising filter |
| written trills | ornament | 2-semitone gate or fast pitch-LFO burst on held steps |
| augmented-dominant swells | lift | pre-chorus pad chord — aug triad, resolve on the drop |
| contrary-motion wedge | cadence | riser + downlifter crossfade into the seam |
| register coda | final chorus | same loop +12/+24, pads to pedal, ceiling voice |

In the orchestral original the run is shared by flute, clarinet and
first violins in relay (the concert reduction gives it all to one
instrument); the relay itself is liftable — pass the running line
between two or three timbres mid-phrase, seamlessly, as a way to keep a
two-minute run from fatiguing one voice.

## 8. Samples & Vocals

Instrumental in every concert form, and the instrumental rule stands.
In the opera the Swan-Bird sings over the opening of the flight — "Ну,
теперь, мой шмель, гуляй, судно в море догоняй…" ("Well now, my
bumblebee, go on a spree, catch up with the ship on the sea") — a
vocal line so incidental it is routinely omitted. Programme/title
material: *Полёт шмеля* (Polyot shmelya), Gvidon, the Swan-Bird
(Царевна-Лебедь), Buyan island, Saltan, Babarikha; the sting scene;
Pushkin 1831.

## 9. Effects & Production Techniques (era analogues)

**Register = filter automation**: the piece's entire loud/soft,
dark/bright arc is played on the pitch axis — trough at G♯3, ceiling at
D7 — where we would sweep a cutoff; the coda is "opening the filter" by
transposition. **Verbatim recap = the drop**: A′ restates 52 bars
unchanged after the ramp; identity through exact repetition (doctrine
rule 1). **Transposition chain = arrangement automation**: development
by +4th steps instead of new material. **Relay orchestration = timbre
crossfade** on one continuous line. **The one dynamic trough** (bare
octaves, b51–54) reads as a mix instruction: strip to sub + lead before
the ramp. Nothing here needs inventing — it needs assigning.

## 10. Emotional Tone & Energy Arc

Mischief, not menace — a scherzo with a story: longing (a son flying
unseen to his father) dressed as comedy. The arc: **fall in** (three
octaves, dominant-lit), **wander** (buzz sentence walking up the
fourths), **duel** (the hammer/trill exchanges — playful aggression),
**sink** (the low trough — the bee far out over the water), **ramp and
return** (the recap as arrival), **climb out the top** (coda to D7 —
gone). It is continuous motion with a composed breath at the trough,
ending cold in minor at maximum altitude. Dread never enters; even at
full density the affect stays light — worth remembering when running
this grammar in a dark track: density reads as energy, not anger,
when the anchors stay consonant.

## What to take, what to leave

- **Take as grammar** (the core haul): the anchor system — full-chromatic
  16th cells pinned to chord tones on downbeats, circling the *fifth*;
  cells a/b + sawtooth + climb as slide-chain vocabulary; the
  transposition-by-4ths development plan; the i↔IV Dorian vamp.
- **Take as engines**: B5 sink-and-ramp (breakdown + written riser), B4
  duel (hammer/trill traded between voices) with its augmented-dominant
  swells, the contrary-motion wedge as seam cadence, the register coda
  (final chorus by relocation), the rising cold exit.
- **Leave**: the opening plunge as a foreground hook (the claimed bit —
  instant gag), any literal fast-chromatic-wiggle statement on a lead
  timbre, novelty-speed framing, sampled anything.

## Open questions → for the notes doc

1. **How chromatic can the 303 line go** before it stops reading as
   acid and starts reading as bee? Bumblebee is 90% semitones; Silver
   Wire's confirmed grammar is scale-bound. Candidate rule: chromatic
   *inside the cell*, anchors strictly diatonic — test where the ear
   flips.
2. **Anchor-on-the-fifth**: adopt as the default for running lines
   (Bumblebee circles the chord's 5th, not the root)? It keeps the root
   free for the bass — convenient division of labour.
3. **The duel**: which second voice trades with the 303 — a second
   303, or a percussive voice (texture role per the Eisgang verdict)?
4. **Register coda vs. fusion payoff**: doctrine rule 7 wants the final
   chorus to fuse two themes; Bumblebee's coda relocates one theme
   instead. Compatible (fuse *and* lift an octave) or choose per track?

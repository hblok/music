## 2. Genre & style placement

A 909originals retrospective describes the track directly: it
"doesn't fit into any particular genre, and in an industry built on
'peaks and troughs,' it has neither." Instead it "embeds itself into
your brain from the off, taking you on a voyage through time and
space." **[verified quote]**

Read against the rest of its history — signed to Väth's Harthouse
(the Frankfurt trance/prog-house label), broken wide via Sasha's
*Global Underground* (a progressive-house/trance DJ-mix franchise),
revived by Maceo Plex (melodic/minimal techno) — the track sits
exactly on the boundary the user's framing implies: **prog
trance crossing into tech-house / early melodic techno**, not a
big-lead trance anthem and not four-on-the-floor minimal techno
either. The 2018 Harthouse/Beatport reissue tags it "Techno (Peak
Time / Driving)" — a modern Beatport genre bucket, almost certainly
retrofitted onto a track from a period (1997) before that taxonomy
existed. **Don't treat the Beatport tag as a genre description of the
original — it's a shelving decision.**

**Practical read:** hypnotic, loop-based, groove-and-texture driven,
genuinely without a scripted peak/drop. This is the single most
important fact for §9 — it puts the track at odds with this repo's
existing trance song-doctrine (`tracks/trance/idea.md`), which is
built entirely around refrain identity and a verse→drop arc.

## 4. Structure

No *timestamped* breakdown exists, but the interview gives a real,
artist-stated shape — for **one specific version**, which matters:

> "...on that version that was released on the [album title garbled by
> auto-caption], there are some parts. So basically it has a long
> intro, then the tonal change. We have the bassline and stuff. And at
> the end there's some [bits] I left... from the early versions... I
> had a minor chord with a string sound and a small phasing [effect]
> on top, and that came back in at the end." **[verified — artist
> interview, paraphrased from auto-caption]**

He then clarifies the **single version differs**: it ends on "this
chord, the string pad" rather than the album cut's returning
minor-chord-plus-phasing passage. So there are at least **two
documented arrangements** of the same underlying track — an album cut
with a reprised minor/phasing passage near the end, and a single edit
with a simpler string-pad ending — and no source here says which one
any given streaming/Beatport listing actually is.

**What this confirms, combined with §2:**
- **Long intro → tonal change → bassline section** is the real,
  artist-stated skeleton — not an invented characterisation.
- **Loop-based and additive**, like the *Dominator* doc's structure
  (§3 there) — not the verse/build/drop shape this repo's other
  trance tracks use. Layers accumulate and recede; the track's
  identity is a *state*, not an arc.
- The ending is a **composed return** (a chord/pad passage reprising
  earlier material), not a cold stop or a scripted final drop —
  consistent with "no peaks and troughs," but it does mean the track
  isn't *formless* either; it has a real beginning/middle/end shape,
  just not this repo's verse/chorus one.

## 5. Bassline & groove — the thing that carries the track

**Confirmed directly by Andreas Krüger**, on camera:

> "For the sequence, for the basic sequence — it's very, very simple.
> It's a TX81Z, the four-operator FM synthesizer... and an MS-10...
> it's doubled — both playing the same line." **[verified — artist
> interview, primary source]**

That's the exact claim the user opened with, now confirmed at the
source rather than inferred from a forum post. What the interview adds
beyond the bare "two synths" fact: this unison pair *is* what Krüger
calls "the basic sequence" — his own name for the track's core, the
one element that "didn't change" across the 20–40 recorded versions
(§1) while everything else (drums, arrangement, overdubs, vocal
fragments) was rewritten around it. **The bass/lead line is not one
part of the track's identity — per the artist, it IS the track's
identity**, composed once and left alone for years of revision.

The interview does **not** mention hand-twisted live filter modulation
on the MS-10 — that detail comes only from the secondary source below,
and should be downgraded accordingly (plausible, era-typical MS-10
performance technique, but not artist-confirmed).

**Secondary corroboration (now superseded as the primary source, kept
for the filter-modulation detail):** a 2008 KVR Audio forum post by
user "tonjaeger" (Berlin, joined that day, 2 total posts) answering a
"what synth is this?" question, adding that **the MS-10 was
hand-modulated by twisting its knobs** (live analog filter/pitch
tweaking, not automation). **[unconfirmed]** — treat this one
additional detail as plausible, not as fact-checked; it was right
about the two synths, which is reason to take it seriously, but it
remains an anonymous forum claim for everything beyond that.

**What this means musically:** there is no separate "bassline" and
"lead" — one unison MIDI line is doubled through two completely
different synthesis engines (analog subtractive + FM), and the
analog half is *played*, not programmed — its filter motion is a live
performance gesture, which is exactly the kind of continuous,
non-repeating micro-variation that makes a long loop-based track stay
interesting without a scripted arc. **The groove IS the
composition here** — there is no separate melodic hook to write.

## 6. Synths & sound design

### Korg MS-10 [verified — Wikipedia]
- 1978, monophonic, 32-key, ~$450 new, sold 1978–1981.
- **One VCO** (triangle/sawtooth/pulse/noise), **one 12 dB/oct
  lowpass filter** (the same filter design as its bigger sibling, the
  MS-20), **one envelope generator**, **one LFO** ("modulation
  generator," routable to VCO pitch or filter cutoff).
- Patch-cable panel on top of normalled connections — CV in/out,
  external signal in, white/pink noise built in.
- Known for "huge sounding electro bass" — a combination of PWM on
  the pulse wave and an aggressive, resonant filter that's
  *hand-sweepable* in real time (no envelope-only automation needed).
- **The "one VCO, one filter" simplicity is the point** — there's
  nothing to detune against itself; all of the character comes from
  filter motion and drive, not stacked oscillators.

### Yamaha TX81Z [verified — soundprogramming.net]
- 1986, 1U rackmount FM module, **4 operators / 8 algorithms** (vs.
  32 algorithms on 6-op FM gear like the DX7), **8 notes polyphony,
  8-part multitimbral**.
- The historically significant feature: **the first FM synth to offer
  operator waveforms beyond a pure sine** (8 waveform choices per
  operator) — this is what gives TX81Z patches their metallic,
  slightly aggressive edge compared to DX7-era glassy FM electric
  pianos and bells.
- Cited users: Astral Projection, Future Sound of London, Josh Wink,
  Squarepusher — all acts associated with harder-edged electronic FM
  timbres. No source located ties a specific TX81Z factory patch
  (e.g. the famous "Lately Bass"/E.Piano presets from the sister DX11)
  to this specific track — don't assert that detail as fact.

### What the pairing actually contributes
Two structurally opposite synths carrying one line:
- **MS-10 half:** subtractive grit, a single pulse/saw oscillator
  through a resonant lowpass that's being hand-swept — warm,
  round-to-snarling depending on filter position, mono, huge low end.
- **TX81Z half:** FM edge — inharmonic-leaning operator content (from
  the non-sine waveforms) gives upper-harmonic bite and a metallic
  transient the MS-10 alone can't produce.
- **Unison of the two = width and character without detuning** — the
  "fatness" doesn't come from multiple analog oscillators beating
  against each other (this repo's usual `1/k**1.3` rolled-saw-stack
  trick), it comes from **two different synthesis methods playing in
  unison**, one continuously modulated by hand.

### Repo translation
Checked `tracks/trance/instruments/README.md`'s bass table — nothing
there matches this shape. The closest existing voice, the "rolling
16th mono bass" (`lost_v6.py:bass_note` lineage: multi-saw stack +
`iirpeak` resonance blend + sine sub, `tanh` drive), is built from
*one* synthesis method stacked for width — the opposite construction
from Hale Bopp's *two-method unison*. **This needs a new bass voice**,
not a re-voiced existing one, built as two independent generators
summed:
1. A single-oscillator subtractive voice (saw or pulse) through a
   resonant lowpass whose cutoff follows a *printable, non-repeating*
   modulation curve per phrase (the "hand-twisted knob" character —
   this repo already has the filter-arc convention in
   `ungeschrieben.py`/`penumbra.py:cutoff_at`, reusable as the
   mechanism even though the sound itself is new).
2. A 4-operator-style FM voice using at least one non-sine modulator
   (a triangle or ramp operator, not just sine-into-sine) for the
   metallic edge — no existing repo module does FM synthesis at all,
   so this is genuinely new ground for the instrument catalog.

No pads/leads/drums research was scoped here — §9 covers what else a
full track would need.

## 7. Effects & production

One concrete detail now exists (§4): the album cut's returning passage
near the end pairs **a minor chord, a string sound, and "a small
phasing [effect] on top."** **[verified — artist interview]** That's
the one specific, sourced effects choice in this doc — a phaser (or
phaser-like modulation) on a string pad, used as a texture for a
*reprise*, not on the bass/lead line itself. Beyond that, no interview
or technical breakdown of the effects chain was found — padding
further with plausible-sounding invention would violate this repo's
sourcing discipline. What else can be said:

- The bass's main "effect" IS the performance — live filter automation
  on the MS-10 half (§5), not a studio delay/reverb trick.
- Genre-typical for 1997 prog-house/trance-adjacent productions:
  tempo-synced delay throws on percussion/FX layers, filtered
  atmosphere pads building the "immersive... voyage" quality the
  909originals review describes. **This is informed inference, not
  a sourced fact about this specific record** — flagged accordingly.

## 8. Emotional tone & energy arc

"Embeds itself into your brain from the off, taking you on a voyage
through time and space" — melodic, immersive, **explicitly not**
built on peaks and troughs. **[verified quote]**

**Shape summary:** a flat, hypnotic plateau rather than an arc —
closer in spirit to a long-form loop-based groove piece than to any
verse/build/drop trance track. This is the opposite energy shape
from every song-doctrine track in `tracks/trance/` (`idea.md`'s
refrain/Q-A/drop model) and closer to *Dominator*'s additive/
subtractive loop structure (§3 of that doc) — minus *Dominator*'s
aggression. Call it: **aggression's hypnotic cousin — same
loop-based non-arc structure, warm/rolling rather than menacing.**

## 9. Translation to this repo

**Genre folder: `tracks/trance/`, not `tracks/house/`.**
`tracks/house/` currently has no `CLAUDE.md`, no instrument catalog,
and only two files (`strike_intro.py`, `strike_variants.py`) — it
isn't a developed genre home yet. `tracks/trance/` has the mature
doctrine, shared instrument library, and verification conventions
this track needs, and the artist's own history (Harthouse signing,
Sasha *Global Underground* placement) is trance-label-rooted even
though the track itself resists the trance *song* doctrine. Land it
in `tracks/trance/` as a **deliberate exception to `idea.md`**, not a
silent violation of it.

**The doctrine conflict, stated plainly:** `tracks/trance/idea.md`
requires refrain identity, verse/chorus Q&A, and a drop. §2 and §8
above establish that Hale Bopp has none of these by design — it's a
loop-based, non-arc, hypnotic groove track. A Hale Bopp-inspired repo
track should **name this exception explicitly in its own notes doc**
(the same way `tech_noir_v3` is flagged in `idea.md` as "a machine
score, not dance music — takes the doctrine without the pop form"),
rather than trying to force a refrain and a drop onto a track whose
entire reference point is refusing both.

**Kit table — what exists vs. what's new:**

| element | library module | fit for Hale Bopp |
|---|---|---|
| bass (the track's actual identity) | none — closest is `lost_v6.py:bass_note` (rolling 16th mono bass) | **wrong shape** (multi-saw stack vs. two-method unison) — new module per §6 |
| atmosphere / immersive bed | `pad_chord` (dark sine pad, every script) or `ungeschrieben.py`'s air bed | reusable as-is for the "voyage through time and space" quality |
| filter-motion mechanism | `ungeschrieben.py`/`penumbra.py:cutoff_at` (printable filter arc) | reusable **mechanism**, new **voice** — apply the existing arc convention to the new MS-10-style oscillator |
| percussion | any 909-family kit in the catalog | plausible starting point; no sourced detail on the original's drum programming, so this is a free choice, not a reconstruction |
| refrain/lead carrier | *(none needed)* | §5/§8: the track has no melodic hook to write — resist the doctrine's instinct to add one |

**Verify additions**, if a track is built from this doc:
- **The exception is declared** — the track's own notes doc states
  up front that it skips refrain/drop by design, citing this doc.
- **The bass unison check** — assert two distinct-timbre generators
  are summed on the same MIDI/note sequence (the "two synths, one
  line" structure is the whole point; a single-generator bass would
  miss it).
- **No scripted drop** — a structural assertion that no section's RMS
  spikes sharply above a rolling average (the anti-arc, stated as a
  measurement, mirroring `silver_wire_v2`'s anti-arc `CUT_PROFILE`

## 10. Calibration — the inspector runs that would settle the open numbers

No reference audio exists yet under `/workspace/music/refs/`. Once a
file is placed there (from *Hale Bopp Mutation*, ideally the "very
first version from April 1997" if isolable, since every other
version is a later remix), from `/repos/music`:

```bash
mkdir -p /workspace/music/refs/hale_bopp
# place the reference file(s), then:
python3 -m inspector.analyse /workspace/music/refs/hale_bopp/hale_bopp.flac \
    --plots --out inspiration/hale_bopp_refs/full.md

# the bassline itself — isolate what two synths in unison actually look like
python3 -m inspector.analyse /workspace/music/refs/hale_bopp/hale_bopp.flac \
    --start 30 --end 90 --separate \
    --stems-out /workspace/music/refs/hale_bopp/bass_stems \
    --out inspiration/hale_bopp_refs/bass_groove.md
```

| question | settled by |
|---|---|
| Real tempo (135? 120? something else on the 1997 original?) | onset/beat tracking on the actual reference, not Beatport/songbpm tags |
| Real key (Bb major vs. F#/Gb — these disagree) | pitch-class histogram on the bass/pad stems |
| Is the bass really two distinct timbres in unison, or does it read as one blended voice? | the `--separate` bass stem, spectral analysis for two harmonic families (subtractive vs. FM-inharmonic) |
| Does the track truly have no RMS-defined "drop"? | per-section RMS across the full length — confirms or overturns §8's "flat plateau" read |
| How much of the "huge bass" is filter motion vs. static tone? | cutoff-frequency tracking over time on the bass stem |

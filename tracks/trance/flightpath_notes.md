# Flightpath — design notes (2026-07-10, next-track plan)

**The bee track — the run at maximum.** Working title *Flightpath*
(the blueprint's own image: "pitch contour as flight path";
alternatives in Q1 — the German naming line is retired per feedback,
2026-07-10). Seed **1900** — the year the bee took off
(premiered 3 November 1900). Blueprint:
`../../inspiration/Bumblebee_RimskyKorsakov.md`, unclaimed by any
track. Queued independently of hammerwerk (different lane: hammerwerk
is the rave shout, this is the run lane's third track).

THE CONCEPT — silver_wire proved the machine can RUN (the acid grammar,
density 0.90, the 112-step run); Bumblebee is the proof that a
maximum-density run can stay a *piece* for two minutes instead of an
exercise. Flugbahn takes the confirmed run grammar and adds the piece
engines the blueprint audited: the anchor system, the
transposition-by-4ths development plan, register as the entire dramatic
axis, the duel, the composed sink-and-ramp, the register coda, the
rising cold exit. The machine-voice arc so far: maschinenherz learned
to sing, silver_wire sang solo, morgenland sang an old song —
**flightpath is the machine in flight**: altitude instead of volume,
the run as the flight path.

**This is a grammar borrow, never a cover** (the eisgang
mechanisms-not-pitches rule, acid dialect): we compose OUR OWN 8-bar
sentence from the blueprint's cell vocabulary (dip&flick, V-turn,
sawtooth, climb) under its anchor rule. The source's literal sentence
is never quoted, and the opening plunge — the radioactive gag surface —
never appears as a foreground hook (its contour is licensed only as a
seam downlifter, see engines).

## Declared up front (not questions)

- **138 BPM, 4/4.** The blueprint's arithmetic is decisive: 16ths at
  138 = 9.2 notes/s; the source's ♩=144 in 2/4 = 9.6. No translation.
- **The refrain** = an 8-bar buzz sentence in the source's form —
  `a b a b | saw climb saw climb′` — Q/A inside the loop: four bars of
  descending cell shapes ask, four bars of chromatic climbs answer.
  Composed once, stated identically (doctrine rule 1).
- **The anti-arc rule holds, and the source hands us the replacement**:
  register IS the filter ("register = filter automation", blueprint
  §9). No track-long cutoff ramp; all development is pitch axis,
  arrangement, and the fourths chain. The cutoff breathes per-phrase at
  a fixed position per section, spread/slope printed and checked.
- **Sparse kit by construction** (blueprint §6: at 9.2 notes/s the line
  IS the subdivision): 909 kick 4/4, offbeat open hat, clap on 2&4,
  crash arrivals — NO closed 16th ghost carpet while the line runs.
  Not the eisgang no-carpet identity (that's a total ban plus the tick
  pair); here the carpet is simply displaced by the melody.
- **Bass = the source's struck-chord behavior**: dark short stab
  quarters on the root (the 303 circles the fifth, the bass owns the
  root — the division of labour from Q4), sub-boom under the kick from
  the big-room master, and in the breakdown a bare sine drone on the
  DOMINANT (the blueprint's naked V pedal). No rolling bass, no
  K-b-b-b — the psy gait stays in silver_wire/maschinenherz's lane.

## What the blueprint gives (mechanisms, never pitches)

- **The anchor system** — the core haul: fully chromatic 16th cells
  pinned to chord tones on every downbeat, circling the chord's FIFTH;
  the flick lands the anchor's chromatic upper neighbour and
  re-descends. Silver wire's accent cycle expressed as *pitch*.
- **The cell vocabulary**: dip&flick (4 down, leap +4th, 3 down),
  V-turn (4 down, 4 back up), sawtooth, climb — slide-chain material
  verbatim (slide the chromatic four, accent the flick).
- **The transposition machine**: no second theme — the sentence
  restates up a perfect 4th with re-harmonized exits. Development plan:
  i → iv → the ♭VII episode → bare V pedal → home (the source's chain
  of fourths, interval-relative).
- **The i↔IV Dorian vamp**: the refrain harmony is i alternating with
  the MAJOR IV — the raised 6th is this track's one borrowed colour
  (the nachtkind-F♯ slot) — closing iv6→V7.
- **The duel** (B4): hammer bars (one-pitch 16th retrigger) and trill
  bars (two-semitone oscillation) traded bar-by-bar between the 303 and
  a second voice (Q5), under augmented-dominant swell pads — the lift
  that commits to no key until the drop lands. DECLARED DISTINCTION
  from maschinenherz's W4a stutter ladder: the hammer stays put and
  trades; it never climbs chromatically into a seam. That move stays
  maschinenherz's.
- **The sink and the ramp** (B5) — the breakdown, composed: register
  trough (line an octave+ down, harmony stripped to the bare V drone),
  then a WRITTEN two-octave chromatic riser in the melody itself — the
  build is pitches, not noise automation. The recap that follows is the
  drop.
- **The register coda** (B6) — the final chorus by relocation: same
  loop, lead +12, pads stripped to a pedal, then the waterfall (the
  plunge cells re-anchored on the ROOT, harmonized i → V7/iv → iv —
  the piece's one secondary dominant, kept as the coda's one spice).
- **The wedge** — contrary-motion chromatic voices fanning outward from
  a unison around a pedal: THE seam device of this track (riser and
  downlifter crossfaded in one gesture). Not nachtkind's reverse
  cymbal, not the era tom fill.
- **The relay** (§7, the orchestral version): the running line passed
  between two timbre variants of the voice mid-phrase, seamlessly — how
  a two-minute run avoids fatiguing one voice (Q7).
- **The rising cold exit**: an unbroken ascent to a single high
  staccato flick, hard stop — the bee leaves the frame upward. Third
  use of the tech_noir cold-end license, first one that exits UP.

## What stays ours

- **The song doctrine**: refrain stated identically and counted (home
  statements only; transposed statements are the development engine,
  printed in the fourths ledger, uncounted); Q/A at melody level
  (cells ask / climbs answer), instrument level (the duel), section
  level (verses ask low, the recap-drop answers full); thesis = the
  first 4 sentence bars, one naked 303, in the first ten seconds
  (never the plunge); bookend per the coda exit.
- **The 303 voice**: flightpath's OWN — the square-core `buzz_note`
  (see "The voice" amendment below; user verdict 2026-07-11: the
  saw-core 303 family all sounds the same across tracks — this one
  gets a new sound). NOT maschinenherz's `acid_note`, NOT
  silver_wire's Q 6 lead, NOT its CUT_PROFILE mechanism or
  register-jump answer device. The within-note bright→dark sweep is
  the one thing every 303 keeps (it is what makes acid read as acid).
- **The big-room master** (directory default): pump (never on the
  303 — it stays bone dry and center, the world pumps around it),
  sub-boom, shelves, tanh bus limiter, FLAC out.
- **Banned as ever**: Dune palette, TTS
  voice, ladders/ice-cracks/shiver stacks
  (maschinenherz's), the literal Bumblebee sentence, the plunge as a
  foreground hook, novelty-speed framing.

## Structure sketch (sections; bars TBD in implementation)

thesis (naked 303, 4 sentence bars) → intro groove → verse 1 (sentence
at home, low register) → verse 2 (up a 4th — the machine starts
walking) → duel 1 + augmented swell (pre-chorus) → **drop 1** (full
sentence home, kit full) → episode (the ♭VII wander, the one
harmonically loose section) → duel 2 (higher station) → **sink-and-ramp
breakdown** (trough → bare V drone → the written riser) → **drop 2 =
the recap** (the source's own lesson: the verbatim return IS the drop;
biggest floor) → **register coda** (final chorus: refrain +12, pads to
pedal, fusion per Q6) → waterfall + exit wedges → rising cold exit.

## Sound-design sketches

- **The line**: `acid_note` family, slides on the chromatic fours
  (a slide chain is sustained motion — the eisgang lesson in acid
  dialect, already proven in silver_wire), accents on flicks and
  anchors. Cells built in code as step lists through a constructor
  that enforces the anchor rule (snap every downbeat to a chord tone)
  and resolves slide chains — the silver_wire `build_half` approach
  re-tooled for anchors instead of accent phase.
- **Hammer bar**: one-pitch 16th retrigger with a per-16th gain cell
  and a rising per-hit cutoff nudge (the roll's melodic cousin).
  **Trill bar**: two-semitone gate figure, fast LFO burst on the held
  step.
- **The relay**: two cached renders of the same voice (bright: LP
  ~2600 / dark: LP ~1800), crossfaded only on cell boundaries, same
  notes — timbre motion with zero grammar smear.
- **Augmented swell pad**: dark sine pad voicing an augmented triad on
  the V, swelling over the last 2 build bars, resolving on the drop
  downbeat.
- **The wedge**: two quiet saw voices starting in unison mid-register,
  fanning ±1 semitone per half-beat around the pedal, one rising one
  falling, crescendo into the seam.
- **The ramp**: the melody itself — a written chromatic ascent ≥ 20
  semitones over ≤ 4 bars on the 16th grid over the bare V drone; a
  snare roll may join the last bar (standard pair), no noise riser.

## The voice — the square-core 303 (2026-07-11, answers amendment)

The extra feedback folded in: maschinenherz, silver_wire and
morgenland all speak through the same saw-core + iirpeak + tanh
recipe, differing only in Q and drive — the ear hears one instrument
across three tracks. Flightpath's line gets the 303's OTHER waveform,
hardware-true and unused by any track: **the square**. `buzz_note` /
`buzz_path`:

- **Pulse core, duty ~0.30** (asymmetric pulse = hollow odd-harmonic
  body with a thin even sheen — nasal, reedy, the buzz), partials
  rolled `1/k**1.25`, sine body 0.30 underneath.
- **A reed formant instead of the acid scream**: moderate resonance
  (Q ~2.5–3, blend ~0.4) parked NEVER — it rides the within-note
  bright→dark sweep (`exp(−t/0.045)`, kept — the acid identity),
  emphasis band ~1.1–1.4 kHz. Drive `tanh(1.0)` — well under
  silver_wire's 1.5. The warmth guardrails all apply.
- **The relay's two timbres are the two orchestral characters**:
  A-bright ("violin" — LP ~2600, formant high, duty 0.30) and
  B-hollow ("clarinet" — LP ~1900, formant low, duty 0.25). Same
  notes, same grammar; crossfade on cell boundaries only.
- **ANTI-GARBLE** (the Q4 caveat + the morgenland v3 lesson): a slide
  chain is ONE note — one attack, one smoothed pitch path
  (`buzz_path`), never a per-16th re-attack inside a chain (the v2
  "broken 303" bug class); detached notes gate at ~0.9; the line is
  bone dry, center, no delay, not pumped — the world moves around it.

Identity separation at a glance (why this is not another
silver_wire): square core vs saw core; C minor vs A minor; the
anchor-on-the-fifth PITCH system vs the accent-phase system; harmony
that TRAVELS (the fourths chain, the Dorian IV, augmented swells, one
secondary dominant) vs one static lap; sparse 909 kit + stab-quarter
bass vs psy kit + K-b-b-b; register as the development axis vs the
fixed CUT_PROFILE; the duel / relay / wedges / sink-and-ramp /
register-coda engines vs the no-break composed trough.

## Verify paragraph (implement exactly)

Section map; seam checklist (wedges named where used); per-section
RMS post-master with the standard song orderings (drop 2/recap ≥
drop 1 on the >120 Hz band if the floors are identical by design;
breakdown is the trough; coda region loudest; outro/exit settles →
hard stop). Refrain statements: home full-sentence count ≥ 6, printed;
the fourths ledger: per-section chain position (i/iv/♭VII/V/i)
printed. THE ANCHOR CHECK: 100 % of the line's downbeat onsets are
chord tones (printed histogram; fifth-of-chord share ≥ 0.5). THE
CHROMATIC BUDGET: semitone share of melodic steps printed, inside the
window agreed in Q3; run endpoints scale tones. Density block: onset
density ≥ 0.85 in run sections, rest budget ≤ 6 per 128 steps,
longest unbroken run ≥ 64, slide-chain count ≥ 4. Register ledger:
per-section median MIDI printed; coda median ≥ chorus 1 median + 12;
the breakdown trough is the global register minimum. The ramp check:
a monotone chromatic ascent ≥ 20 semitones within ≤ 4 bars inside the
breakdown, over the V pedal (printed). Duel check: hammer/trill bars
alternate voices bar-by-bar (printed grid); hammer pitch is stationary
per bar (the anti-ladder assertion). Relay check: crossfade count and
cell-boundary alignment printed. Anti-arc: per-statement cutoff spread
~0 Hz, linear trend slope ~zero. Cold exit: the track's last onset is
its highest pitch; audio ends within 200 ms of it, no fade tail.
Anti-garble block: slide chains rendered as single legato path notes
(chain count + mean chain length printed, zero re-attacks inside
chains by construction); the line dry/center/unpumped by construction
(printed as declarations). Big-room metrics with pinned floors per
the silver_wire_v3 block.

## Open questions for review

1. **Name.** English titles from here on — the German line (nachtkind
   / eisgang / maschinenherz / morgenland) is retired, 2026-07-10;
   existing tracks keep their names. *Flightpath* (recommended — the
   blueprint's own image, and the register axis IS the trajectory) vs
   *Stratosphere* (the blueprint's own name for Coda II — but a worn
   word in trance) vs *Vector*. Nothing bee-literal — naming the gag
   invokes it. Seed 1900 either way.
   Answer: Flightpath is good.

2. **Key.** C minor (recommended — unclaimed in the directory, and the
   source's own ♭VII-episode key; chain: Cm → Fm → B♭m episode → G
   pedal → Cm; the Dorian vamp is Cm↔F, the A♮ is the borrowed colour)
   vs D minor (the verified edition's key — but tech_noir's claim) vs
   B minor (the opera's waypoint — but brushes lost's loop).
   Answer: C minor good

3. **The chromatic budget** (blueprint Q1: where does acid flip into
   bee?). Recommended rule: chromatics FREE inside the cell (the four
   16ths between downbeats), downbeat anchors strictly chord tones,
   run endpoints scale tones; semitone share window **0.50–0.80** of
   melodic steps — deliberately under the source's 0.90, well over
   silver_wire's scale-bound line. The v1 listen decides if the window
   moves; the gag lives in the plunge contour + literal quote, which
   are banned regardless. Alternative: stricter (chromatics only as
   lower neighbours on weak 16ths — i.e. silver_wire's budget, safer
   but less bee).
   Answer: Ok, chromatics free inside cell

4. **Anchor on the fifth** (blueprint Q2). Adopt as this track's
   default for the running line (recommended: keeps the root free for
   the stab bass — clean division of labour; the flick's upper
   neighbour target comes with it)? Or anchor on mixed chord tones
   (root/3rd/5th, freer, less source-true)?
   Answer: ok, default run line - however, make sure the 303 does not sound garbled.

5. **The duel partner** (blueprint Q3). (a) RECOMMENDED — the stab
   voice answers: the source's own soloist-vs-accompaniment duel; the
   bass/chord stab echoes the trill notes between hammer hits; keeps
   silver_wire's register-jump device untouched. (b) A second, darker
   303 an octave down — needs a declared silver_wire borrow. (c) A
   percussive voice — consistent with the eisgang verdict (percussive
   = texture) since hammer/trill IS texture, but risks reading as a
   drum fill, not a duel.
   Answer: Yes, answer sounds interesting.

6. **Register coda vs fusion payoff** (blueprint Q4; doctrine rule 7).
   Recommended: BOTH — the final chorus lifts the refrain +12 (the
   relocation) while the duel voice sounds its answer material under
   it for the first time (the fusion). The two devices are compatible:
   fuse AND lift. Alternative: pure relocation (source-true, simpler,
   but breaks doctrine rule 7 and would need a declared deviation).
   Answer: OK Both.

7. **The relay.** Adopt the two-timbre crossfade as this track's
   signature device (recommended — it is the orchestral version's own
   anti-fatigue trick, unclaimed, and it gives the long runs motion
   the anti-arc rule forbids the filter from giving)? Or one timbre
   throughout (simpler; silver_wire survived on one voice at
   density 0.90 — but over 16-bar, not two-minute, spans)?
   Answer: ok, two-timbre.

Extra: In the previous tracks, we've used the 303 as voice many times. However, it always sounds exactly the same. We can afford to tune the 303 a bit, so that has a different sounds more fitting to this track, and does not repeat what we've heard in all the previous (silver_wire, maschinenherz, eisgang, etc).
